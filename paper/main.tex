\begin{abstract}
This document records the data, extraction methods and independent replication of
an HST ACS/WFC longitudinal trailing observable. The observable replicates across
two additional pairs at each of eight historical epochs, but a latent radiation
damage state and cross-mission predictive validation have not been established.
No radiation-causation claim or validated forecast is reported.
\end{abstract}

\section{Data}
The provenance contract separates archival observations, genuinely published
calibration measurements and synthetic truth. MAST observation-query snapshots
define a deterministic paired-dark ACS/WFC pilot. Original files are retained in
a content-addressed local cache; source identifiers, retrieval times, software
revisions, processing state, usage notes and cryptographic hashes accompany them.
The release cohort is defined by the versioned machine-readable RAW product plan,
not by a newly evaluated archive search.
The historical cohort contains 48 exposures: one development pair and two
replication pairs at each of eight epochs from 2003 through 2024. Six August 2025
exposures form a checksum-pinned temporal holdout whose pixel arrays remain
unopened.

NASA SPDF OMNI hourly files from 2003--2025 and NOAA GOES-R SGPS five-minute files
from November 2020--2025 form the continuous environment layer. OMNI particle
fluxes are observed external-environment measurements. The SGPS greater-than-10-MeV
quantity is a band-integrated proxy constructed from observed differential channels
and the greater-than-500-MeV integral channel. Neither is detector dose. Missing
entries and the April--October 2020 particle gap remain missing. Granular public
Gaia CTI and Euclid trap-pumping time series
have not been obtained. Public VIS imaging products and published calibration
constraints are different evidence classes \citep{pagani2026,skottfelt2024,q1vis}.
\section{Detector observables}
\subsection{Paired RAW-dark pilot}
Only individual full-frame dark exposures meeting the frozen time and exposure
criteria are selected. Array geometry is checked against the image headers and
each chip is oriented away from its parallel readout register. Because calibrated
gain entries are unpopulated in RAW headers, the pilot retains native digital
units and commanded gain as an explicit stratum.

The background is estimated from symmetric same-row side pixels. Persistent,
isolated peaks are required in both exposures. The observable is a downstream
minus upstream trail sum divided by the local peak signal. Leading, serial and
offset-column blank statistics are retained. Signal and transfer-distance bins
are fixed in the versioned method contract. Column-cluster bootstrap intervals
describe population sampling uncertainty, excluding calibration systematics.

This observable does not measure a physical trap density. Pixel bias structure,
readout effects, gain calibration, detector temperature and selection effects
remain unresolved. A CALACS/reference-calibrated comparison is required before
physical interpretation. The approach is informed by established warm-pixel and
capture/release methods \citep{anderson2010,massey2026}; it does not reproduce their
full calibrations.
\subsection{Official calibration and operating-state comparison}
Copies of the same RAW exposures are processed by the official ACSCCD stage,
using a pinned HSTCAL environment and CRDS context. Bias and overscan calibration
and detector gain conversion are applied, with the resulting geometry, units and
calibration switches checked explicitly. Dark, CTI and flash corrections are
omitted. The intermediates retain deferred charge and illumination effects and
are not fully calibrated science products. Source, reference, executable, log
and output hashes accompany each derived image.

The comparison uses separately frozen electron signal bins and excludes flagged
neighbourhoods except for hot/warm-pixel flags. Its selected population therefore
differs from the RAW native-unit pilot. Differences between the two summaries do
not isolate a causal calibration effect. Matched support files preserve both WFC
temperature channels without assuming an active-sensor mapping. Exposure headers
record post-flash duration and status, commanded gain and shutter position.
Background, temperature, electronics era and between-exposure variation remain
necessary nuisance variables. The initial serial statistic measures signed
array-axis asymmetry; opposite amplifier directions can cancel serial trails.
\subsection{Analytic injection validation}
Independent images with known exponential trails test extraction, sign convention,
blank controls and rejection of transient single-frame peaks. The injection is
labelled synthetic and is not a trap-occupancy simulation. It cannot establish
physical parameter recovery for a future latent-state model.
\section{Model}
The present exposure layer provides only an unfit low-dimensional basis. Both
mission domains contain log-transformed particle flux and inverse F10.7 as a
solar-modulation proxy. The HST/LEO basis additionally interacts particle flux
with normalized Kp; the Gaia/Euclid L2 basis does not. No coefficient, temporal
lag, shielding kernel, effective detector exposure or physical dose is estimated.

\section{Validation}
The replication screen was versioned before replication pixels were summarized.
It requires four positive pair-index-by-chip time slopes whose 95\% epoch-bootstrap
intervals remain positive. A Bonferroni family of 32 simultaneous blank-control
intervals uses 20,000 column-cluster resamples per interval. These checks assess
direction and control behaviour; they do not replace a pair-aware nuisance and
measurement-error model. The temporal holdout remains sealed until that model,
its baselines, metrics and failure thresholds are versioned.
\section{HST results}
The replication primary selection contains 102,604 peak measurements. The four
slopes range from 0.00981 to 0.01328 in trail fraction per year, with all four
bootstrap intervals excluding zero on the positive side. No blank control fails
the preregistered familywise screen. Across 16 epoch-by-chip comparisons, the
development value and mean replication value have Pearson correlation 0.9678,
mean absolute difference 0.01944 and root-mean-square difference 0.02302. All 32
separate development/replication interval comparisons overlap.

This replicates a longitudinal detector observable, not a radiation-dose response.
The replication outcome has Spearman correlation 0.9424 with calendar year and
0.8559 with measured background; post-flash, gain and engineering state also vary
with epoch. These unadjusted associations expose confounding and are not treated
as causal tests. The physical-inference gate therefore remains closed. Exact
values are generated in \texttt{results/hst\_replication/assessment.json},
\texttt{development\_comparison.json} and \texttt{nuisance\_summary.json}.
\subsection{Historical forecast baselines}
Six preregistered models were evaluated in four expanding-window folds, each
holding out all four pair-by-chip strata at one epoch. The calendar-linear B0
model has the lowest RMSE, 0.04024. The event-plus-background B4 model has RMSE
0.04055 and the lowest mean Gaussian negative log predictive density, $-1.7824$,
compared with $-1.7692$ for B0. The cumulative-particle B3 model has RMSE 0.07093.
Because neither exposure model beats B0 on both RMSE and predictive density, the
preregistered exposure-support screen fails. The physical-inference gate remains
closed.
