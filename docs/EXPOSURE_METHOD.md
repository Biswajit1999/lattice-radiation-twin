# Space-environment and mission-response method

This layer keeps four quantities distinct: measured external environment, transformed proxy, inferred effective exposure and physical dose. The present implementation covers only the first two. It is not a GEANT4 or SPENVIS transport calculation, and no proxy is labelled as silicon displacement-damage dose.

NASA SPDF OMNI2 supplies a continuous hourly activity record from 2003 through 2025. The solar-wind component combines geocentric and L1 measurements, while the energetic-particle component is a documented sequence of IMP and GOES sources. The parser follows the annual ASCII formats encountered in the archive, converts documented fill values to nulls, and retains Bz GSM, solar-wind density and speed, Kp, ap, Dst, AE, sunspot number, F10.7 and integral proton channels. OMNI documents its energetic-proton coverage as ending on 2020-03-04, although activity indices continue. The selected 2003 file first contains valid proton flux on 2003-08-14. All 133,113 retained proton hours carry magnetospheric-contamination flag `-1`, which the documentation defines as “not checked” after day 306 of 1988. They are not described as contamination-free. Missing proton values remain null. Source: [OMNIWeb data documentation](https://omniweb.gsfc.nasa.gov/html/ow_data.html), accessed 2026-09-10.

NOAA NCEI GOES-R SEISS/SGPS Level-2 five-minute files extend the particle record. The frozen plan selects every available daily science file for GOES-16 in 2020–2024 and GOES-18 in 2025, preserving monthly directory listings, unavailable months and superseded filenames. The GOES-16 archive begins in November 2020, leaving an explicit April–October gap after OMNI proton coverage ends on 2020-03-04. Four March 2021 daily files are absent upstream.

SGPS reports thirteen differential proton channels nominally spanning about 1–500 MeV plus an integral channel above 500 MeV. The files' literal upper differential bounds are 404 MeV for GOES-16 and 390 MeV for GOES-18. The derived series integrates only the reported interval portions above 10 MeV, adds the greater-than-500-MeV integral channel and averages east/west reconstructions when their contributing validity counters are complete. It therefore omits unreported energy gaps and is labelled a **band-integrated proxy**, not an exact measured greater-than-10-MeV integral. Historical files use `L2_SciData_TimeStamp`; later files use `time`. Both schemas and masked validity counters are tested. The result retains 541,399 valid samples out of 543,456 possible five-minute slots from November 2020 through December 2025. Sources: [NOAA GOES-R SEISS L2 dataset](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/metadata/landing-page/bin/iso?id=gov.noaa.ncei.swx:seis-l2-goesr) and [SEISS product page](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/goes-r-space-environment-in-situ), accessed 2026-09-10.

The NOAA S1 event threshold is at least 10 proton flux units for particles at or above 10 MeV. Contiguous above-threshold OMNI hours form observed screening events. The same numerical threshold applied to the SGPS reconstruction forms proxy screening runs; these are not official NOAA S1 event classifications. Event duration and fluence use the nominal one-hour or five-minute cadence, terminate across missing samples, and are not detector damage. Source: [NOAA Space Weather Scales](https://www.spaceweather.gov/noaa-scales-explanation), accessed 2026-09-10.

Initial mission-response terms are an unfit basis rather than a single asserted transfer function. Both domains receive `log1p(>10 MeV flux)` and an inverse-F10.7 galactic-cosmic-ray proxy. The HST/LEO basis additionally contains an interaction with normalized Kp as a geomagnetic-activity term. The Gaia/Euclid L2 basis does not contain this LEO term. Every coefficient, lag and shielding response must be learned inside each forward-chaining training split. The inverse-F10.7 term is a solar-modulation proxy, not a measured GCR flux. HST and L2 coefficients remain mission-specific.

Monthly products contain uncorrected observed fluence, coverage, separate cumulative OMNI and SGPS series, three-month rolling fluence only when every month has at least 80% coverage, F10.7 differences, rolling ap, threshold markers and time since the last source-specific threshold run. No fluence is divided by coverage. Solar-cycle number and months since minimum use the SILSO minima for cycles 23–25; this is a calendar coordinate, not a dose model. Source: [SILSO cycle minima](https://www.sidc.be/SILSO/cyclesmm), accessed 2026-09-10.

There is no empirical OMNI–SGPS overlap in the frozen standard archive. Their levels are not spliced, ratio-scaled or accumulated together. The monthly table selects the available series for convenience but retains both original cumulative quantities. Inferred effective exposure and physical dose remain unestimated, and the physical-inference gate remains closed.
