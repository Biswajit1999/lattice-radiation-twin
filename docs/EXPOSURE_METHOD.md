# Space-environment and mission-response method

This layer keeps four quantities distinct: measured external environment, transformed proxy, inferred effective exposure and physical dose. The present implementation covers only the first two. It is not a GEANT4 or SPENVIS transport calculation, and no proxy is labelled as silicon displacement-damage dose.

NASA SPDF OMNI2 supplies a continuous hourly, near-Earth/L1 activity record from 2003 through 2025. The parser follows the official 55/57-word format, converts documented fill values to nulls, and retains Bz GSM, solar-wind density and speed, Kp, ap, Dst, AE, sunspot number, F10.7 and integral proton channels. OMNI documents its energetic-proton coverage as ending on 2020-03-04, although activity indices continue. Missing proton values after that date remain null. Source: [OMNIWeb data documentation](https://omniweb.gsfc.nasa.gov/html/ow_data.html), accessed 2026-09-10.

NOAA NCEI GOES-R SEISS/SGPS Level-2 five-minute files extend the particle record. The frozen plan selects every available daily science file for GOES-16 in 2020–2024 and GOES-18 in 2025, preserving monthly directory listings, unavailable months and superseded filenames. The GOES-16 archive begins in November 2020, leaving a documented gap after OMNI proton coverage ends on 2020-03-04. SGPS reports differential proton bands from about 1 to 500 MeV plus an integral channel above 500 MeV. The derived greater-than-10-MeV series requires every contributing band, integrates the portions above 10 MeV, adds the greater-than-500-MeV integral channel and averages east/west sensors where valid. It is retained as a distinct instrument series until an overlap calibration is fitted using only training dates. Sources: [NOAA GOES-R SEISS L2 dataset](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/metadata/landing-page/bin/iso?id=gov.noaa.ncei.swx:seis-l2-goesr) and [SEISS product page](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/goes-r-space-environment-in-situ), accessed 2026-09-10.

The NOAA S1 event threshold is at least 10 proton flux units for particles at or above 10 MeV. It will define event markers, not detector damage. Source: [NOAA Space Weather Scales](https://www.spaceweather.gov/noaa-scales-explanation), accessed 2026-09-10.

Initial mission-response terms are an unfit basis rather than a single asserted transfer function. Both domains receive `log1p(>10 MeV flux)` and an inverse-F10.7 galactic-cosmic-ray proxy. The HST/LEO basis additionally contains an interaction with normalized Kp as a geomagnetic-activity term. The Gaia/Euclid L2 basis does not contain this LEO term. Every coefficient, lag and shielding response must be learned inside each forward-chaining training split. The inverse-F10.7 term is a solar-modulation proxy, not a measured GCR flux. HST and L2 coefficients remain mission-specific.
